import pandas as pd
from prophet import Prophet
from prophet.plot import plot_plotly
import plotly.express as px

# (Assuming master_absences is already loaded in memory)

# ==========================================
# 1. CALCULATE OVERALL AVERAGES PER SEASON
# ==========================================
# Get total squad sizes per season across all Top 6 teams
team_squads = master_absences.groupby(['season', 'team_name'])['player_name'].nunique().reset_index(name='squad_size')
season_squads = team_squads.groupby('season')['squad_size'].sum().reset_index()

# Get total injuries per season across all Top 6 teams
injuries_df = master_absences[master_absences['player_status'] == 'Absence/injury']
season_injuries = injuries_df.groupby('season').size().reset_index(name='total_injuries')

# Merge and calculate the average
ts_df = pd.merge(season_squads, season_injuries, on='season', how='left')
ts_df['total_injuries'] = ts_df['total_injuries'].fillna(0)
ts_df['avg_injuries'] = ts_df['total_injuries'] / ts_df['squad_size']


# ==========================================
# 2. FORMAT DATA FOR PROPHET
# ==========================================
# Prophet strictly requires columns named 'ds' (datestamp) and 'y' (metric)
# We convert "2019-2020" -> "2019-08-01" (Approximate start of the season)
ts_df['ds'] = pd.to_datetime(ts_df['season'].str[:4] + '-08-01')
ts_df['y'] = ts_df['avg_injuries']

prophet_df = ts_df[['ds', 'y']].copy()

print("[*] Training Prophet Model on historical injury data...")

# ==========================================
# 3. TRAIN PROPHET & FORECAST
# ==========================================
# Initialize model (disabling smaller seasonalities since our data is yearly)
m = Prophet(yearly_seasonality=False, weekly_seasonality=False, daily_seasonality=False)
m.fit(prophet_df)

# Create future timestamps for the next 3 seasons (2026, 2027, 2028)
future_years = [2026, 2027, 2028]

# Predict the future trend

# ==========================================
# 4. PLOT WITH PLOTLY
# ==========================================
# Use Prophet's native Plotly integration for beautiful interactive graphs
fig = plot_plotly(m, forecast)

fig.update_layout(
    title='Prophet Forecast: Average Gameweeks Missed per Player (Top 6 Premier League)',

Researching backing the increase in injuries over time

The Escalation of the Global Epidemiological BurdenOver the past two decades, the incidence and cumulative burden of specific injury typologies have surged dramatically across elite European football. The UEFA Elite Club Injury Study, which aggregates data across 21 consecutive seasons (2001/02 to 2021/22) from top-tier European clubs including EPL participants, reveals a profound epidemiological shift. Hamstring injuries, which represent the most ubiquitous and disruptive affliction in professional football, accounted for 12% of all reported injuries during the 2001/02 season. By the culmination of the 2021/22 season, this proportion had doubled to 24%. Correspondingly, the percentage of total injury absence days—a direct metric of the injury burden and matches missed—caused by hamstring pathologies increased from 10% to 20% over the identical timeframe.Recent short-term longitudinal analyses further underscore this acceleration. Between the 2014/15 and 2021/22 seasons, the incidence of hamstring injuries sustained during training sessions increased by 6.7% annually, while the associated injury burden (absence days) increased by an alarming 9.0% annually. 1 Concurrently, the incidence of hamstring injuries sustained during competitive match play rose by 3.9% annually. 1 This data unequivocally demonstrates that not only are injuries occurring more frequently, but their severity and the resulting time lost to rehabilitation are compounding year over year. (Gemini: Sources: https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/5/292)

The empirical data across all available longitudinal vectors irrevocably confirms that the absolute rate of injuries, the severity of these injuries as measured by total days lost, and the consequent matches missed per individual player have escalated significantly over the past two decades in the English Premier League.

There are not much papers highlighting the link between match congestion vs player injuries. The following review looks at 8 articles speaking about this topic and have found the following:

  • Congested fixture schedules expose players to increased match injury incidence
  • However, layoff duration was typically lower during congested periods
  • One study identified a lower training injury incidence during congested periods

Research does show that rest days are decreasing over time with the prem players showing an average of just 3.5 recovery days between matches.

“The correlation between match proximity and injury risk is highly sensitive. Epidemiological studies reveal that when a subsequent match is played within 24 hours of a previous fixture, the injury risk spikes by an astronomical 3.8 to 6.8 times compared to matches preceded by a standard 3-to-7-day recovery break. Even a 3-day recovery window is strongly associated with an increased incidence of both general injuries and specific lower extremity trauma. Evidence suggests that a minimum of five days is required for complete neuromuscular and central nervous system recovery following a high-intensity professional match. However, the reality of the EPL schedule dictates that players in top leagues average merely 3.5 days between matches. Furthermore, players operating in high-intensity positional roles—such as wide forwards, attacking midfielders, and fullbacks—who are subjected to two or more matches within a rigid four-day window exhibit a 28% higher risk of sustaining soft tissue injuries.”

import pandas as pd

# 1. CALCULATE AVG INJURIES PER PLAYER
# ==========================================
squad_sizes = master_absences.groupby(['team_name', 'season'])['player_name'].nunique().reset_index(name='squad_size')

injuries_df = master_absences[master_absences['player_status'] == 'Absence/injury']
injury_counts = injuries_df.groupby(['team_name', 'season']).size().reset_index(name='total_injuries')

inj_stats = pd.merge(squad_sizes, injury_counts, on=['team_name', 'season'], how='left')
inj_stats['total_injuries'] = inj_stats['total_injuries'].fillna(0)
inj_stats['avg_injuries'] = (inj_stats['total_injuries'] / inj_stats['squad_size']).round(2)

# ==========================================
# 2. INJECT ACTUAL LEAGUE FINISHING POSITIONS
# ==========================================
# Historical league positions for the Top 6 teams (19/20 through 23/24)
# Note: 24/25 is excluded as the season is not finished yet.

So now, what is causing the increase in injuries??

Research highlights that a key metric to answer this question is ACWR:

“The Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR)The correlation between total match volume and injury is governed physiologically by load management metrics, most notably the Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR). The ACWR is calculated by dividing the acute workload (typically the total distance covered or high-speed running executed in a trailing 1-week period) by the chronic workload (the 4-week rolling average of the acute workload).Spikes in the ACWR—which occur almost exclusively during congested fixture periods where acute match volume rapidly outpaces the player’s baseline chronic physiological fitness—are heavily correlated with muscle strains and joint trauma. A significant majority of hamstring injuries, for example, occur when the mean ACWR in the week prior to the injury exhibits a rapid, unmanaged fluctuation. This demonstrates a crucial nuance: it is not merely the total cumulative number of matches over a nine-month season that causes injury, but the density and rapid accumulation of match minutes within concentrated micro-cycles without proportional chronic adaptation.”
