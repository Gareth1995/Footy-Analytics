Research highlights that a key metric to answer this question is ACWR:

The Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR), defined as the correlation between total match volume and injury is governed physiologically by load management metrics, most notably the Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR). The ACWR is calculated by dividing the acute workload (typically the total distance covered or high-speed running executed in a trailing 1-week period) by the chronic workload (the 4-week rolling average of the acute workload).Spikes in the ACWR—which occur almost exclusively during congested fixture periods where acute match volume rapidly outpaces the player's baseline chronic physiological fitness—are heavily correlated with muscle strains and joint trauma. A significant majority of hamstring injuries, for example, occur when the mean ACWR in the week prior to the injury exhibits a rapid, unmanaged fluctuation. This demonstrates a crucial nuance: it is not merely the total cumulative number of matches over a nine-month season that causes injury, but the density and rapid accumulation of match minutes within concentrated micro-cycles without proportional chronic adaptation."
